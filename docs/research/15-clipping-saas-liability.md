# Research: clipping-saas-liability

_Research date: 2026-08-15/16 (SaaS-pivot sweep). Requirements, prices, and timelines are time-sensitive — recheck official sources before relying on them._

## Summary

Going multi-tenant changes Winz's legal posture in four ways: (1) it becomes an OSP that must build the full DMCA 512 stack (designated agent: $6 online registration, renew every 3 years; a repeat-infringer policy that is actually enforced; notice-and-takedown) — while understanding 512(c) cleanly covers content stored/processed on Winz servers but NOT the outbound copies Winz pushes onto TikTok/YouTube/Instagram, where the real shields are the volitional-conduct doctrine and the new, very defendant-friendly SCOTUS Cox v. Sony (Mar 2026) contributory standard (liability only for inducement or a "service tailored to infringement"); (2) competitors (OpusClip, Kapwing, StreamLadder, Eklipse) uniformly push all rights responsibility onto the user via warranties (and, for OpusClip, indemnification) and none verify ownership — OpusClip's ToS is the model to copy; (3) server-side Kick VOD ripping as a service is categorically worse than doing it for yourself: it creates Winz-owned direct-infringement copies with no safe harbor, and bundling Cloudflare-bypass converts a personal-use gray area into potential §1201(a)(2) circumvention "trafficking" ($200–$2,500 statutory per act; 20+ such suits filed by May 2026) — safer architectures are user-OAuth via Kick's official API (live since ~Mar 2025), user-uploaded recordings, or browser-extension capture in the user's own session; (4) music claims land on the CUSTOMER's social account (Content ID / TikTok audio scanning), and Winz should mirror competitors in disclaiming it. Launch basics: home-state LLC ($35–$500, usually 1–5 business days online; BOI/CTA reporting permanently dead for domestic LLCs as of FinCEN's Aug 2026 final rule), Termly/iubenda-generated ToS+Privacy Policy with lawyer-reviewed custom clauses, 13+ COPPA age gate (amended COPPA rule fully effective Apr 22, 2026), and yes — a free beta still needs full terms, a privacy policy (platform APIs require one), the DMCA agent, and AS-IS beta disclaimers.

## Key facts

- DMCA designated agent: register online in the Copyright Office DMCA Directory, $6 per designation/amendment, MUST renew every 3 years (no auto-renewal); required for 512 safe harbor from day 1 of processing third-party users' content, even in a free beta.
- 512 safe harbor also requires an adopted, reasonably implemented, and user-communicated repeat-infringer termination policy — BMG v. Cox shows a paper policy you don't enforce forfeits the safe harbor entirely; 'repeat infringer' means repeatedly accused/flagged, not court-adjudicated.
- 512(c) covers storage at user direction plus automated related functions (transcoding, reformatting, playback per Viacom/YouTube and UMG/Veoh lines) — but it does not clearly cover the copies Winz's servers upload TO TikTok/YouTube/Instagram; for those, protection comes from (a) volitional-conduct doctrine (VHT v. Zillow, 918 F.3d 723 (9th Cir. 2019); Cablevision — the user who presses the button supplies volition) and (b) Cox Communications v. Sony (U.S. Mar 2026, unanimous): contributory liability now requires inducement or a service 'tailored to infringement'; mere knowledge that customers infringe is not enough — this reversed a $1B verdict and is highly favorable to neutral clipping tools.
- Competitor ToS: OpusClip is the gold standard (user warrants all rights, broad user indemnification, DMCA agent + repeat-offender termination clause, NCS-only music license, deepfake ban); Kapwing has rights language but no indemnity/DMCA clause in ToS; StreamLadder (Dutch law) has essentially nothing — no indemnity, no DMCA, only 'user solely responsible for obtaining legal permission' in its acceptable-use policy; Eklipse's terms are thinnest of all (no indemnity or DMCA policy found; it even grants itself promo rights over user videos). NONE verify ownership — the industry norm is warranty + indemnity + notice-and-takedown, zero proactive verification.
- Kick capture as a service: Kick ToS bans scraping/automated access; server-side ripping for customers = Winz makes the infringing copy itself (no 512 protection for Winz's own copying) and bundling Cloudflare/bot-detection bypass invites DMCA §1201(a)(2) trafficking claims — 20+ suits filed by May 2026 over YouTube downloaders and bot-evasion (Sony v. Uncharted Labs, S.D.N.Y. Apr 15 2026 — rolling-cipher claim survived; Yout v. RIAA — downloader lost in D. Conn., 2d Cir. appeal pending; but Ziff Davis v. OpenAI — robots.txt is not a TPM). §1201 has NO safe harbor and carries $200–$2,500 statutory damages per act. Safer architectures: user OAuth via Kick's official API (dev.kick.com, OAuth 2.1 + PKCE, public since ~Mar 2025), user uploads own recording, or browser-extension capture running in the user's own authenticated session.
- Music in customer clips: enforcement is automated and lands on the CUSTOMER's account (TikTok audio scanning on every upload — strikes/bans for repeats, policy updated Apr 2025 and LIVE-music rules tightened Jul 2025; YouTube Content ID claims). Winz's own exposure is secondary-liability only, now hard to establish post-Cox unless Winz induces or tailors the service to infringement; competitors disclaim it entirely and make the user warrant rights.
- Launch basics: form the LLC in the founder's home state ($35–$500 filing; WY $100/$60-yr, DE $90–110 + $300/yr franchise tax; online approval often 24h–5 days; registered agent $0 self / $50–$300/yr); BOI/CTA reporting permanently eliminated for U.S.-formed companies (FinCEN interim rule Mar 21 2025, final rule effective Aug 14 2026). ToS/Privacy generators actually used: Termly (~$10–15/mo), iubenda ($3.49–$5.99/mo tiers), TermsFeed (one-time ~$14+/doc) — but the load-bearing clauses for this product (content warranty, indemnity, DMCA, music disclaimer, beta terms) should be custom/lawyer-reviewed, using OpusClip's ToS as the category template.
- COPPA: amended rule effective June 23, 2025 with full-compliance deadline April 22, 2026 — expanded 'personal information' (incl. biometric identifiers like voiceprints/face templates, relevant to AI clipping), separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosures, written children's data-security program, retention limits. Standard mitigation for Winz: 13+ age gate at signup, don't direct the service to children, ToS age representation (consider 18+, since contracts with minors are voidable and Kick/TikTok posting tools skew adult).
- Free beta changes little legally: you still need ToS + Privacy Policy (TikTok/Google/Meta developer programs contractually require a privacy policy; CalOPPA requires one for any site collecting Californians' PII), still need the DMCA agent and repeat-infringer policy, and should add beta-specific AS-IS/no-warranty/no-SLA disclaimers, liability caps, feedback-license and modification/termination-at-will clauses; 'beta' labels don't waive FTC Section 5, privacy, or copyright obligations, and gross-negligence waivers are unenforceable in California.

## Gotchas

- The 512 safe harbor does NOT cover the step everyone assumes it does: Winz's own servers ripping a Kick VOD is Winz's direct copying (no 'storage at the direction of a user'), and Winz's upload onto TikTok's servers is outside 512(c)'s text too — safe harbor protects the storage/processing layer; the capture and outbound layers are protected only by architecture (user-directed automation, OAuth-verified ownership) and the post-Cox secondary-liability standard.
- A registered DMCA agent with an unenforced repeat-infringer policy is worthless — BMG v. Cox stripped the safe harbor for exactly that; and the $6 registration silently expires every 3 years if not renewed.
- Marketing copy is legal evidence: after Cox v. Sony (U.S. 2026), the main way a neutral tool becomes liable is inducement — a single landing page saying 'clip any streamer's best moments' converts the strongest defense into the plaintiff's Exhibit A. Market only 'your stream, your clips.'
- §1201(a)(2) trafficking has NO safe harbor, no notice-and-takedown cure, and $200–$2,500 statutory damages per act — bundling Cloudflare bypass into a customer-facing capture feature is a categorically different risk than one user running a script, and the customer's consent doesn't cure it because the TPM belongs to Kick/Cloudflare, not the streamer. Yout v. RIAA's 2d Cir. appeal was still undecided as of mid-2026, so the district-court holding (no download button + ToS = effective TPM) is the operative precedent.
- User indemnification clauses (OpusClip-style) are worth including but near-worthless in practice against individual creators with no assets — treat them as deterrence/allocation language, not real protection; the real protection stack is Cox + volitional conduct + fast takedown + termination logs.
- Eklipse's ToS is a cautionary tale in both directions: it omits every protective clause (no warranty, no indemnity, no DMCA policy) AND contains a customer-hostile 'promotional assets' clause claiming perpetual promo rights over all processed videos — don't copy either mistake; OpusClip is the template.
- Don't pay for BOI/CTA compliance: FinCEN permanently exempted U.S.-formed companies (final rule effective Aug 14, 2026), but formation services still upsell it.
- Free beta traps: 'beta' waives nothing (FTC §5, COPPA, DMCA all fully apply); California voids gross-negligence waivers; and the amended COPPA rule now covers voiceprints/face templates — Winz's AI pipeline processes both, so the 13+ age gate isn't optional; contracts with minors are voidable, favoring an 18+ gate.
- Several key sites block automated fetching (kick.com and eklipse.gg return 403 to bots — quotes for those came via search snippets and a proxy fetch of eklipse.gg/terms-of-use); re-verify exact clause text manually before quoting in legal documents.

## Full details

# Legal architecture for Winz as a multi-tenant clipping/reposting SaaS (US, 2026)

## 1. DMCA §512 safe harbor — the multi-tenant delta

Single-tenant Winz had no OSP posture; multi-tenant Winz stores and processes video "at the direction of a user," which makes §512(c) both available and necessary.

**Designated agent (17 U.S.C. §512(c)(2)):**
- Register online via the Copyright Office DMCA Designated Agent Directory (dmca.copyright.gov). **Fee: $6 per designation** (and $6 per amendment/resubmission). Confirmed current per Copyright Office FAQ.
- **Renewal required every 3 years** — the registration expires if not renewed/resubmitted ($6 again). The Office sends a reminder but tracking it is your responsibility; an expired registration silently forfeits the safe harbor.
- Also required: post the agent's contact info + your DMCA policy on the Winz site (a /dmca or /copyright page), and process notices "expeditiously."

**Repeat-infringer policy (§512(i)) — the condition companies actually lose on:**
- Must be (1) adopted, (2) reasonably implemented, (3) communicated to users. *BMG v. Cox* (4th Cir. 2018) stripped Cox of the safe harbor because it had a "thirteen-strike" policy it never really enforced (terminated then reactivated subscribers). "Repeat infringer" means repeatedly *accused* via valid notices — not adjudicated by a court.
- Practical implementation for Winz: strike counter per customer account, documented termination at N strikes (2–3 is typical), no routine reactivation, logs of every notice and action. Enforce it even during free beta.

**Does the safe harbor cover Winz's outbound posting to TikTok/YT/IG? Partially — this is the key structural point:**
- **Covered:** storage of the customer's VOD/upload on Winz servers, and automated "related functions" — courts read §512(c) to include transcoding, reformatting, clipping-at-user-direction, thumbnail/variant generation, and playback (*Viacom v. YouTube*; *UMG v. Shelter Capital/Veoh*; recently extended to automated format standardization and algorithmic display).
- **Not clearly covered:** the copy Winz's servers push onto TikTok's/Google's/Meta's servers. §512(c) shields infringement "by reason of the storage" on *your* system; the copy that ends up on TikTok resides on TikTok's system (TikTok gets its own safe harbor for it — you don't). No case squarely blesses a scheduling/cross-posting tool under 512(c).
- **The actual shields for the outbound act:**
  1. **Volitional conduct / causation** for direct infringement: an automated pipeline that renders and posts only what the *user* selected and connected is like Cablevision's RS-DVR and Zillow's auto-processing — "the person who presses the button" (the customer) supplies volition (*Cartoon Network v. CSC Holdings* (2d Cir. 2008); *VHT v. Zillow*, 918 F.3d 723, 732 (9th Cir. 2019): automatic processing/trimming/padding is "insufficiently volitional"). Design consequence: keep a human customer decision in the loop for publishing (or an explicit standing instruction the customer configures), and don't have Winz staff curate/select content.
  2. **Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment (U.S., decided Mar 2026, unanimous)** for secondary liability: SCOTUS threw out the "knowledge + material contribution" theory. Contributory infringement now requires that the defendant **induced** infringement or **"sold a service tailored to infringement."** Merely knowing some customers infringe — even being "indifferent" — is not enough (reversing a $1B verdict). A general-purpose clipping tool marketed to streamers for *their own* streams sits comfortably outside this, **as long as marketing never suggests clipping other people's content** ("clip any streamer!" copy would be Grokster-style inducement evidence).
- Net: register the agent and run the 512 program (it fully protects the storage/processing layer and is table stakes), but treat *marketing discipline + user-directed automation + fast takedown/termination* as the real protection for the reposting layer.

## 2. How competitors allocate the "customer clips someone else's stream" risk (actual ToS, fetched Aug 2026)

**OpusClip (opus.pro/terms-of-service) — the category's best-drafted terms; use as template:**
- Warranty: "You represent and warrant you have all rights necessary to do so, in the manner in which you contribute it," plus a specific warranty that User Submissions won't "infringe any third party's copyrights, Publicity Rights or any other rights."
- Indemnity: user indemnifies "the OpusClip Parties … from and against any and all claims, liabilities, damages (actual and consequential), losses and expenses (including attorneys' fees)" arising from use of the Services.
- DMCA: designated-agent notice process (legalnotices@opus.pro), counter-notice with 10–14 business-day restoration, and an explicit right to "remove and discontinue Services to repeat offenders."
- Music: only licenses NCS (NoCopyrightSounds) library music, revocable at will; all other audio is user's problem ("Any information or Content … is the sole responsibility of the person from whom such Content originated").
- Also bans deepfake/likeness misuse. **No ownership verification of source video anywhere.**

**Kapwing (kapwing.com/policies/terms-of-service):** users retain ownership, grant Kapwing a hosting/reformatting license; "If you post content that you did not create, you must have the right to do so … public domain, used under license … or falls under the fair use doctrine." Notably **no indemnification clause and no DMCA/repeat-infringer section in the ToS itself**, and no verification.

**StreamLadder (streamladder.com/policies/terms-of-service, last updated May 2021, Netherlands law):** startlingly thin — no rights warranty in the ToS, no indemnity, no DMCA procedure; the acceptable-use policy makes the customer "solely responsible for all material they input … and for obtaining legal permission to use any works included." Consequential-damages disclaimer only. Shows the enforcement reality: nobody has sued these tools yet, but this is not the posture to copy.

**Eklipse (eklipse.gg — site blocks bots; terms-of-use retrieved via proxy):** the thinnest of all — no user rights warranty, no indemnity, no DMCA policy, no ownership verification, nothing on VOD capture or auto-posting in the terms. One clause to note (and NOT copy): Eklipse grants itself the right to use "all of your videos processed by Eklipse … at any time and in its sole discretion" as "promotional assets."

**Industry answer to "do they verify ownership?": No one does.** The universal model is: user warranty + (best practice) indemnity + notice-and-takedown + repeat-infringer termination + zero proactive review. That is also what §512 assumes (no duty to monitor, §512(m)).

**Winz ToS should therefore include:** all-rights warranty covering *imported/connected* content specifically (not just uploads); indemnification incl. attorneys' fees; DMCA policy + agent + counter-notice; repeat-infringer termination; music/third-party-audio disclaimer; a content policy banning clipping channels the user doesn't own/control (and consider a lightweight ownership signal — e.g., only allowing capture from a Kick channel the customer has OAuth-verified as their own, which is both a legal talking point and a product moat competitors lack).

## 3. Kick-side: VOD capture *as a service* vs. for yourself

Doing it for yourself (single-tenant) was a personal ToS breach + weak copyright exposure. Doing it **for customers at scale** changes the analysis in three ways:

1. **Direct infringement with no safe harbor for the capture step.** When Winz's servers rip a Kick VOD, *Winz* makes that copy. If the customer targets someone else's channel, Winz is the direct infringer of the capture copy — §512(c) doesn't apply (the material wasn't stored "at the direction of a user" in the protected sense if Winz's own automated ripper fetched it from Kick; and courts treat the service's own copying as outside the harbor). Statutory damages: $750–$30,000 per registered work, up to $150,000 willful (17 U.S.C. §504(c)).

2. **§1201(a)(2) "trafficking" — the serious new risk.** Kick fronts with Cloudflare bot protection; a service that *bundles* bypass (TLS fingerprint spoofing, solver services, rotating residential IPs) and offers it to the public looks like providing a circumvention service. Landscape as of mid-2026 (Norton Law, May 14, 2026): **20+ lawsuits** assert §1201 claims against automated video downloading/scraping. Key data points: *Yout LLC v. RIAA* (D. Conn. 2022 — YouTube downloader *lost*; the court found the absence of a download button + ToS restrictions constituted an effective TPM; 2d Cir. appeal No. 22-2760 still pending); *Sony Music v. Uncharted Labs* (S.D.N.Y. Apr 15, 2026 — rolling-cipher circumvention claim survived dismissal); *Cordova v. Huneault* (N.D. Cal. Jan 23, 2026 — same); but *Ziff Davis v. OpenAI* (S.D.N.Y.) dismissed a 1201 claim because robots.txt doesn't "effectively control access." Whether Cloudflare-style general bot protection is a TPM "protecting a copyrighted work" is unresolved and circuit-split-ish (Ticketmaster v. RMG accepted CAPTCHA as a TPM). Crucial: **§1201 has no §512 safe harbor, no innocent-infringer out, and carries $200–$2,500 statutory damages per act of circumvention (§1203(c)(3))** — per-clip-fetch multiplication is the nightmare scenario. And unlike the copyright claim, *the streamer's own consent doesn't clearly cure it* — the TPM belongs to Kick/Cloudflare, not the streamer.

3. **Contract/tort exposure concentrates on the vendor.** Kick's ToS prohibits "copying, distributing, or disclosing any part of the Service … including by any automated or non-automated 'scraping'" and "using any automated system, including 'robots,' 'spiders,' 'offline readers.'" A customer breaching this risks their channel; a SaaS doing it for thousands of customers is a commercial target for breach-of-contract/tortious-interference claims and platform-level blocking (Kick banning Winz-linked activity or its customers).

**Safer architectures, ranked:**
- **(a) User OAuth via Kick's official API** — Kick opened a public developer API (~March 2025, dev.kick.com, OAuth 2.1 + PKCE, scoped tokens, plus a $100K developer fund). Whatever content endpoints expose (channel/VOD references), acting within a user-granted OAuth scope is authorized access: no circumvention, no scraping breach, and it structurally proves the customer controls the channel being clipped. Build here first.
- **(b) Customer uploads their own local recording** (OBS local record / Kick's own download of their VOD). Winz is a pure 512(c)-style processor of user-submitted files — the OpusClip/Kapwing posture. Zero Kick exposure.
- **(c) Browser-extension / client-side capture** running in the *customer's* authenticated session. The customer, not Winz, makes the copy on their own machine; Winz never touches Kick's servers, and there's no bundled circumvention (the user's own logged-in browser is not "circumventing" anything). ToS breach risk stays personal to the user.
- **(d) Server-side ripping with Cloudflare bypass — avoid as a productized feature.** If retained at all, restrict to OAuth-verified own-channel capture, and even then prefer (a)–(c).

## 4. Music in customer clips

- **Where enforcement actually lands:** on the *customer's* destination account. TikTok runs automated audio scanning on every upload plus a rightsholder reporting channel (IP policy updated Mar 27/effective Apr 26, 2025; LIVE-content music rules tightened ~Jul 2025 to approved/royalty-free only); repeats → strikes → permanent ban of the customer's account. YouTube Content ID claims/blocks the Short (revenue redirect or block, strikes for formal takedowns); Meta's Rights Manager similarly. The service that rendered the clip is invisible to these systems.
- **Winz's legal exposure** for rendering+posting a clip containing the customer's in-stream music is secondary-liability only (the customer directed the processing). Post-*Cox* (Mar 2026) that requires inducement or a music-infringement-tailored service — a neutral clipper that happens to pass through stream audio does not qualify. Direct-infringement framing fails on volitional conduct (automated processing at user direction, *VHT v. Zillow*). Residual risk: don't add features that *select for* music content, don't market "keep the music," and honor 512 notices against clips stored on Winz.
- **Competitors' handling:** yes, they disclaim it. OpusClip: content is "the sole responsibility of the person from whom such Content originated," and its only affirmative music license is the revocable NCS library. StreamLadder/Eklipse: silent (all risk on user by default). Winz ToS should (i) state customer is solely responsible for audio in source content, (ii) warrant/indemnify accordingly, (iii) if Winz ever adds a background-music library, license it properly (NCS-style or Epidemic/Artlist API) and scope the grant to clips made in-product.

## 5. Business basics for launch

**LLC — form it before public beta** (an LLC shields personal assets from exactly the copyright/1201 claims above; copyright statutory damages are the single biggest solo-founder risk in this category):
- **Home state is usually right** for a bootstrapped SaaS (forming in DE/WY while operating elsewhere forces double registration/fees). Filing fees range **$35 (MT) – $500 (MA)**; typical $50–$200. Wyoming: $100 filing + $60/yr report. Delaware: $90–$110 filing + **$300/yr flat franchise tax** (only worth it if raising VC — and VCs want a DE *C-corp* anyway, so don't pre-optimize).
- **Timeline:** online filings approve in **24 hours–5 business days** in most states (DE ~24h; some states 2–3 weeks by mail). Registered agent: self = $0; commercial = **$50–$300/yr** (Northwest ~$39 first year bundled). Stripe Atlas-style bundles ~$500 if you want banking/EIN handled.
- **BOI/CTA: dead for you.** FinCEN's interim final rule (Mar 21/26, 2025) exempted all U.S.-formed companies from beneficial-ownership reporting, and the **final rule effective Aug 14, 2026** made that permanent — only foreign entities registered in a US state still report. Ignore formation-service upsells for "BOI compliance."

**ToS + Privacy Policy:**
- Generators practitioners actually use: **Termly** (free tier; ~$10–15/mo paid; best US-state-law coverage), **iubenda** ($3.49–$5.99/mo starter/essentials; best for EU/multilingual), **TermsFeed** (one-time ~$14+/document, no subscription). Any of these produces the 80% (privacy policy, cookie policy, generic ToS skeleton).
- The 20% that must be custom for this product (generator templates won't have it): all-rights content warranty for *connected/imported* streams; indemnification; DMCA agent + repeat-infringer clause; music/third-party-audio disclaimer; social-account authorization clause ("you authorize Winz to publish to accounts you connect"); AI-processing disclosure; beta terms; arbitration/class-waiver + governing law. Cheapest sound path: generator baseline + flat-fee startup-lawyer review (~$500–$2,000), cribbing structure from OpusClip's ToS.
- Privacy policy is **contractually mandatory** regardless of law: TikTok Developer Terms, Google API Services User Data Policy (YouTube), and Meta Platform Terms all require a posted privacy policy to keep API access — your distribution pipeline dies without one. CalOPPA independently requires one for any site collecting Californians' PII. State comprehensive privacy laws (CCPA etc.) have revenue/volume thresholds Winz won't hit at launch — but write the policy honestly now (data collected, OAuth tokens, retention, processors).

**COPPA / age gates:**
- Amended COPPA Rule: **effective June 23, 2025; full compliance required by April 22, 2026** (now in force). Changes: "personal information" now includes **biometric identifiers (voiceprints, face templates)** — note Winz's AI processes faces/voices, another reason not to serve under-13s; separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosures/targeted ads; written children's information-security program; data-retention limits; (plus a Feb 25, 2026 FTC policy statement easing consent for age-verification data specifically).
- Practical: **neutral age gate at signup, 13+ minimum, ToS representation of age, don't market to minors.** Better: require 18+ (contracts with minors are voidable; billing later needs capacity; the connected platforms' business/creator tooling skews 18+). Winz is not child-directed, so full COPPA machinery is avoided by gating + not knowingly collecting under-13 data — but honor deletion if you learn otherwise.

**Free beta — what actually changes:** almost nothing waivable.
- **Still need:** ToS (this is where the liability caps, content warranty, license to process/post, and arbitration live — you need them *most* when the product is roughest), privacy policy (platform APIs + CalOPPA), DMCA agent + repeat-infringer policy (safe harbor has no "beta" exception; "free" doesn't matter to §512 — though no subscription revenue *does* helpfully weaken the "direct financial benefit" prong of vicarious liability).
- **Add beta-specific clauses:** conspicuous AS-IS / no-warranty (merchantability, fitness, non-infringement disclaimed); no SLA/uptime/data-durability promise; right to modify, wipe data, or terminate the beta at will; feedback license (all feedback assignable/usable royalty-free); liability cap (e.g., $100 or fees paid); optional confidentiality for private betas. Caveats: "beta" labeling does not exempt you from FTC Act §5 (deceptive "free" claims, misrepresenting data practices), and California voids waivers of gross negligence/willful misconduct — cap, don't purport to eliminate.
- **When billing starts:** add auto-renewal disclosures (state ARL laws + FTC click-to-cancel landscape), refund terms, and price-change notice — flag for a second legal pass at monetization, not at beta.
